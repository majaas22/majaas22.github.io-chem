Introduction

Introduction and Project Aims

Background

Reactions in which products and reactants are interchangeable are known as reversible reactions. These reactions take place under specific conditions such as pressure, temperature and concentration. Dynamic equilibrium is reached when the forward and reverse reactions occur simultaneously and at equal rates, causing the concentrations of reactants and products to remain constant over time, although they are not necessarily equal. Understanding equilibrium is essential for predicting the final composition of a reacting system and for controlling reaction conditions efficiently.

Mathematical models are used by scientists to describe and analyse how the concentrations of reactants and products change over time in reversible reactions. These models allow reactions to be investigated without the need for extensive experimental work, saving both time and resources. Computational simulations also reduce the impact of human error and experimental inaccuracies, such as measurement errors or material loss, leading to more precise results. As a result, computational modelling provides a more reliable method for testing hypotheses and analysing reaction behaviour.

Aims

  • To develop a computational model of a reversible chemical reaction using Python.
  • To investigate how variations in forward and reverse rate constants affect reaction rate and equilibrium position.
  • To extend the model to include a multi-step reaction mechanism and analyse its impact on reaction behaviour.
  • (EXTENSION) To investigate the effect of temperature on reaction rate using the Arrhenius equation.

Hypotheses

H₁ - For a reversible reaction, the concentrations of reactants and products will change over time until dynamic equilibrium is reached, at which point the concentrations remain constant.

H₂ - Introducing an irreversible reaction step into a reversible system will shift the equilibrium position by continuously removing intermediate species, leading to increased overall conversion of reactants.

H₃ (EXTENSION) - Increasing temperature increases reaction rates according to the Arrhenius equation and alters reaction behaviour.

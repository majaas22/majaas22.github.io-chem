Model and Method

Model and Method

Overview of the Model

The python simulation models how concentrations of chemical species change over time in a closed reaction system. Three simulations were developed: the first models a simple one step reversible reaction A ⇌ B, the second introduces an irreversible step B → C to create a multistep reaction mechanism and the third further extends the model by taking into consideration the effect of temperature using the Arrhenius equation.

In these simulations, time is treated as the independent variable while the concentrations of each chemical species are the dependent variables. The control variables consist of the rate constants, time step size (Δt), initial concentrations of the chemical species and temperature (in simulations one and two). As a result a set of values, showing how the concentration values change over time, is obtained. This is the output of the simulation and the values are then used to generate concentration-time graphs for each of the reactions.

Time Dependent Modelling of Concentration

In this model, the concentration of each chemical species is treated as a function of time: concentrations of each species are not constant, but instead constantly change as the reaction progresses. A system of differential equations is used to describe the rate of change of concentration for multiple variables simultaneously with respect to time. The equations are coupled as the rate of change of each species is dependent on the other concentrations within the reaction system. This system of equations links the concentrations’ effect on each other and allows for the overall behaviour of the reaction to be described and modelled mathematically.

Mathematical Formulae

As mentioned above, the reaction system can be described using coupled differential equations that show how the concentrations of each species change over time as the reaction progresses. For simulation one and the first step of simulation two and three (A ⇌ B), the rate of change of concentrations can be written in the form

d[A]/dt = -k1[A] + k2[B]

d[B]/dt = k1[A] - k2[B]

Then in simulation two and three, an additional irreversible step (B→C) is introduced, written in the form

d[B]/dt = k1[A] - k2[B] - k3[B]

d[C]/dt = k3[B]

Where k1, k2, k3 are rate constants and [A], [B] and [C] represent the concentrations of each species.

Numerical Implementation - Euler's Method

As differential equations are used to model a rate of change, they cannot be solved to give discrete values; therefore a numerical method is required. In this project, Euler’s method is used to calculate the concentration values at discrete time steps throughout the reaction. The general formula used to update concentration values is

New Conc = Current Conc + (d[species] / dt) x Δt

where Δt is the time step. The general formula is the same for every species unlike the differential equations which are dependent on each other. The process is repeatedly carried out until multiple concentration values are obtained using the equation over various values of time. This method is applied to each species in the reaction to obtain approximate values of concentration and time, which can then be used to plot concentration-time graphs, and analyse the behaviour of the chemical system.

Simulation Design

The model consists of 3 simulations. Simulation 1 mimics a simple reversible reaction A⇌B. This initial model allowed me to ensure that equilibrium can be reached and to observe the basic behaviours of the system such as how the concentration of the chemical species changes as equilibrium is approached. Most importantly, the simplicity of this version allowed me to ensure that the math and numerical methods were working as expected before I added more complex steps to the model.

Simulation 2 expanded on this by introducing an additional irreversible step B→C. Adding this step allowed for a multistep reaction, where a product intermediate is formed, to be simulated. Due to the formation of the intermediate and the irreversible step, this reaction can no longer reach a state of equilibrium. This can be observed on the concentration-time graphs generated as the concentration of B reaches a peak and then decreases as C is formed.

Temperature dependence is incorporated into simulation 3 by varying the rate constants through the Arrhenius equation. This resulted in the rate constants no longer being constant but instead varied with temperature. This extension to the model allows for the effect of temperature on reaction rates to be investigated and analysed. Concentration-time graphs with varied temperature values were generated and compared and it was found that the higher the temperature, the quicker the reaction progressed.

Model Assumptions

This model makes many assumptions:

  • The reaction takes place in a closed system meaning that the transfer of matter in and out of the system is prevented and therefore the mass remains constant throughout the entire reaction process.
  • The system is homologous: there are no concentration gradients as the concentrations of all species are uniform.
  • The rate constants are assumed to remain constant throughout the entire reaction in simulations one and two. In simulation 3, rate constants vary with temperature.
  • In simulations one and two, temperature remains fixed and its effect on the rate of reaction is not considered.
  • No side reactions occur: only the desired reactions take place.
  • The time step (Δt) in Euler’s method is assumed to be a very small value in order to give accurate values and prevent rounding errors which could arise from greater time step values.

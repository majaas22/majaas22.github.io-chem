Conclusion

Overall Findings

All three simulations successfully showed how different factors can influence the behaviour of a reaction system. Simulation 1 demonstrated that a reversible reaction can reach dynamic equilibrium with the concentrations of the chemical species becoming constant towards the end of the reaction. Simulation 2 introduced an irreversible step which continuously removed the intermediate to produce the final product. Simulation 3 investigated how a change in temperature affects the chemical system and concluded that increasing temperature increases the rate of reaction allowing the reaction to reach its final state faster. Overall, all three simulations were consistent with the expected principles of chemical kinetics and supported the general trends that were predicted by the hypotheses.

Evaluation of Hypotheses

The results from Simulation 1 support the hypothesis ‘For a reversible reaction, the concentrations of reactants and products will change over time until dynamic equilibrium is reached, at which point the concentrations remain constant.’. The concentration time graph generated by the model displayed that the concentrations of both A and B changed rapidly at first before gradually levellin g off and becoming constant, showing that the forward and reverse reactions continued happening but both at the same rate. At the point where the concentrations of the chemical species become constant, dynamic equilibrium has been reached. Therefore, the results support the hypothesis.

The results from Simulation 2 support the second hypothesis: ‘Introducing an irreversible reaction step into a reversible system will continuously remove intermediate species, causing a greater proportion of reactants to be converted into products.’ Introducing an irreversible step into the equation removed the intermediate B from the reversible system, meaning that B could not remain at a constant concentration in the same way as in Simulation 1. As the simulation progressed, both A and B were eventually almost eliminated to a concentration of 0, while C increased to its maximum concentration. These results support the hypothesis.

The results from Simulation 3 support the final hypothesis: ‘Increasing temperature increases reaction rates according to the Arrhenius equation, causing the reaction to reach completion more quickly.’ Increasing the temperature led to faster changes in the concentrations of the chemical species. At 350K the concentration of C was still rapidly increasing at the end of the simulation; whereas at 375K it began to level off. At 400K, C had reached its maximum concentration within the same time period as the two previous temperatures, demonstrating that the reaction had progressed quicker. This is consistent with Arrhenius equation as increasing the temperature increases the rate constants, therefore increasing the reaction rate. The results obtained support the hypothesis.

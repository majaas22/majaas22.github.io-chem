Limitations

Limitations

Although the simulations successfully reproduced the expected behaviours and supported the hypotheses investigated, they relied on several assumptions which simplified the model down and reduced the accuracy of the results. These assumptions made the model easier to build and interpret results but they also reduced the ability to recreate real life reaction systems accurately.

Limitation 1: Euler's Method

The simulations were solved using Euler’s method which is a simple numerical method used to estimate solutions to differential equations when an exact, discrete value cannot be found. It is straightforward to implement into the model and suitable for modelling change of concentration over time; however it does not produce an exact solution. Instead, the concentration is updated over time by using small time intervals. This means that the accuracy of the result is dependent on the size of the time step chosen as a smaller time step.. If the time step is too big, a less accurate graph could be produced as the slope would be updated less frequently. This could potentially affect the predicted time for the reaction to reach equilibrium. A small time step of 0.1 was used throughout the simulations to minimise this error, ensuring that the concentration curves were smooth and followed the expected behaviour. Despite this, Euler’s method is still an approximation and a small degree of error remains and cannot be fully avoided.

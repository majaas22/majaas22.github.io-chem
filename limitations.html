Limitations

Limitations

Although the simulations successfully reproduced the expected behaviours and supported the hypotheses investigated, they relied on several assumptions which simplified the model down and reduced the accuracy of the results. These assumptions made the model easier to build and interpret results but they also reduced the ability to recreate real life reaction systems accurately.

Limitation 1: Euler's Method

The simulations were solved using Euler’s method which is a simple numerical method used to estimate solutions to differential equations when an exact, discrete value cannot be found. It is straightforward to implement into the model and suitable for modelling change of concentration over time; however it does not produce an exact solution. Instead, the concentration is updated over time by using small time intervals. This means that the accuracy of the result is dependent on the size of the time step chosen as a smaller time step.. If the time step is too big, a less accurate graph could be produced as the slope would be updated less frequently. This could potentially affect the predicted time for the reaction to reach equilibrium. A small time step of 0.1 was used throughout the simulations to minimise this error, ensuring that the concentration curves were smooth and followed the expected behaviour. Despite this, Euler’s method is still an approximation and a small degree of error remains and cannot be fully avoided.

Limitation 2: Perfect Mixing Assumed

The model assumes that throughout the simulation the reaction mixture is perfectly mixed meaning that physical properties of the mixture,such as temperature and concentration, stay uniform throughout. It also would mean that every particle in the mixture has the exact same probability of having a successful collision and reacting, which simplifies the calculations by allowing the concentration to be defined as a single value that changes with time.

In real life, chemical reactions do not occur under these conditions, instead concentration gradients develop. Variations in concentration can affect the frequency of successful collisions. Incorporating a mixing effect would help produce a more accurate representation of the reaction and produce better results; however it would be significantly more complex and time consuming to implement, so the assumption was used as a compromise.

Limitation 3: Constant Conditions Assumed

The models were coded to occur under constant conditions. This meant that pressure, temperature and volume remained fixed in the three simulations. Although Simulation 3 investigated temperature dependence by comparing the same reaction at different temperatures, the temperature remained constant throughout each simulation and did not change over time.

In real life chemical systems, conditions fluctuate. Temperature changes as reactions are always either endothermic or exothermic as heat is taken in or released due to changes in bond energies which causes the temperature to vary as the reaction progresses. Pressure and volume can also vary throughout a reaction for example due to the number of gas molecules produced. While these changes may not sound significant, they can influence the behaviour of the system such as increased temperature increasing the rate of reaction. Assuming constant conditions, the calculations can be simplified but do not accurately reflect how real chemical systems behave.

Limitation 4: Simplified Reaction Mechanism

The model only involves one irreversible step followed by a reversible step. This simplifies the mechanism and allows for the investigation of the fundamental principles of chemical kinetics, such as dynamic equilibrium and temperature dependence, while also keeping the model straightforward and analyse.

In real life, most reactions involve several steps and multiple intermediates. Side reactions are also very common, producing additional products that were not accounted for in this model. These processes can affect the reaction rate and amount of product formed and therefore a simplified mechanism cannot fully represent the complexity of real chemical systems.
